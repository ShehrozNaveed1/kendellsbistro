A little French. A lot of character.

Kendells sits opposite Leeds College of Music, part of the city's arts quarter alongside the Playhouse, BBC Leeds and Northern Ballet. Inside, it's a casual, informal place built around one idea: quality food, wine and service, in a proper French bistro atmosphere.

No one tops up your glass after every sip, or hovers with an oversized pepper mill. Cloth napkins, but not starched. Candles flickering in wax-covered bottles. An open-plan kitchen you can watch, and a blackboard on the wall that gets rewritten most nights.

The Story

No fuss. Just good food, wine and company.

Kendells was never meant to be a designer restaurant — Steve says as much himself. It's his own take on a Parisian bistro: a bit mismatched, built around the rustic places he loves finding by accident in French villages, and cooked from a lifetime spent eating his way around the country.

He doesn't claim to be the best chef in Leeds — that title, he'll tell you, belongs to his mum. What he offers instead is a kitchen that changes its mind daily, chalked up on the board rather than laminated on a menu, and a room that's more interested in your evening than in impressing you.

The Room

Come for the food.
Stay for the atmosphere.

Wooden tables set close together, candles burning down into wax-covered bottles, and a kitchen you can watch work through the pass. It's intimate without trying too hard to be romantic — the kind of room that suits a first date or a Tuesday with old friends just the same.
